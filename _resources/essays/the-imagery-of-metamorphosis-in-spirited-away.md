Miyazaki's (2002) _Spirited Away_ is an incredible film and contains a great deal of insight and illuminating imagery. One central storytelling technique used throughout the movie is that of metamorphosis and transfiguration. In this short essay, I will discuss consumerism and mutual aid. I will link the story and narrative of these characters to our own lives and world.

One major downfall of current culture that could destroy our world is our fixation on consumption. It is all too easy to want more and more in our lives. To feel like we can't get enough. Whether it is money, objects, or even experiences as a human race, we seem to be able to upset the world's natural balance with our desire for more. This need for more and the associated greed shows itself inside the spirit world of the bathhouse. Its savagery and desperateness are seen in the insatiable appetite of No-Face as he tries to devour everything and everybody. It is also portrayed in the Bathhouse workers who are willing to do everything and anything to cater to No-Face's gold (which later turns to dust). The movie starts with another scene that is also full of consumerism. We see Chihiro begging her parents not to eat the spirit's food (although she doesn't know it is from the spirit world). Her father dismissively says to Chihiro, "don't worry, you've got daddy here, he's got credit cards and cash" (Miyazaki, 2002, 09:07) as they are gulping down the food and are turned into actual pigs.

Chihiro has to show both self-resilience and engage in mutual aid throughout the movie to get her parents turned back to their human form. A number of sequences show a world of complexity and interdependence (Miyazaki, 2002). One of these that I found very satisfying is Yubaba's baby Boh's transformation into a mouse and her harpy into a tiny bird. Prior to this, we see Boh as an emotional baby (not only a giant physical one) threatening to hurt Chihiro if she didn't play with him. In this transformation, we see these two characters have to work together to travel to visit Yubaba's sister's house. During this experience, Boh grows and learns self-reliance as they help one another.

This thread of personal growth also goes on throughout this feature film. We see Chihiro develop and grow. Even Yubaba seems to have a different attitude and approach to others at the end of the movie. If we as a society and as individuals are going to change our consumeristic and capitalistic ways, I would argue that there is a need for us to all grow individually and support each other. It will take working together and helping one another to make a significant change. It might be challenging and feel awkward at first. But, like baby Boh, hopefully, we can learn to grow and develop personally, be able to advocate for others, and just maybe enjoy our new form so much that we could change back but choose not to.
