The last year of my studies has facilitated my growth towards my planned dissertation. During the Spring 2020 intensive, I felt privileged to see Andrea Montgomery Di Marco defend her dissertation. This experience sparked the desire to use Participatory Action Research (PAR) as my methodology. Throughout my coursework in Ecology of Ideas, and especially as I started to draft an outline of my paper, I relied heavily on her study and the format to consider my project. Being tasked with reviewing a dissertation's methodology, I wanted to continue to branch out and find other scholarly exemplars.

Montgomery Di Marco (2020) wrote about PAR from a feminist perspective and addressed an immigrant population. During the first semester at CIIS, I read through several dissertations, which was incredibly useful for understanding what the Ph.D. is and defining a dissertation. Still, they did not have any shared focus other than the general area of study. They were all about trauma, resilience, and youth with problematic histories. Honing in on a more specific area of study, I was able to find and review dissertations from Hall (2020) and Riemenschneider (2014) that used Participatory Action Research within educational settings. Both studies' material is engaging and has been a useful process for me in considering my methodology. Hall's (2020) research and methods feel very comprehensive. The author also incorporated the review of a large amount of archival data and interviews along with the group work.

The focus of this review will be on Riemenschneider's (2014) study. She focuses her research on professional development through a PAR process with a small group of school personnel. Her research also feels more policy-focused than using PAR as a methodology for implementing the professional development, which is more along the lines I see my research forming. There is still a remarkable amount of crossover between our areas of research.

Like much of my consideration of PAR, Riemenschneider (2014) describes Freire (1970/2000) as a foundational thinker in considering the theory surrounding the framework. She connects learning theories such as reflection, collaboration, and empowerment, job-embedded supports, and adult learning principles to praxis and adult learning. She lays out aspects such as the significance of the study and its limitations.

In offering a broad overview of what PAR is for her study, Riemenschneider (2014) defines that it "features a collaborative approach for facilitating change and empowering marginalized populations. In this case, a research framework was employed for paraeducators that was designed to offer guidance, assistance, and support" (p. 21).

One compelling aspect of the study is the change that took place during the study. Riemenschneider (2014) evolved her research cycle and way of considering collaboration and reflective conversations within the process of her research. Her sample size was five, and much smaller than I am considering having. In my project, I'd like to have about 20 participants. The participants were also composed of a wide range of persons and made up more like a coalition. A couple of paraeducators were involved. I was also surprised to see a teacher, a vice-principal, and the district superintendent all involved. Seeing a superintendent engaged in such a small working group was especially surprising for me. The overall amount of "working time" wasn't identified; my impression was it was completed through a couple of meetings and some interviews. It was not the potentially 12 one-and-a-half to two-hour sessions I am considering.

In looking at the focus, I'd like to dive deeper into this dissertation. I engaged in more of a brief reading of it and not an in-depth and considered review. My initial feeling about the study's focus, which I would call policy-focused as opposed to direct-intervention focused, is that it is excellent to have both types of work out there. I hope that my research grows upward within organizations versus trickling down from policy changes. In actuality, the most effective changes would likely come from having a dual focus.

Riemenschneider's (2014) study appears to be rigorous and has been a useful intervention. It has also opened my eyes to the possibility of doing a bit less within my research. I don't know if I am ready to change my expected research project drastically, but it shows that I could have a smaller scope to work with, which might be beneficial.
