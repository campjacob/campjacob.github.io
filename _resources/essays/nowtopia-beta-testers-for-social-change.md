Carlsson and Manning (2010) define Nowtopia[^1] as work done for social and ecological reasons and not for capital attainment. Small groups and parts of society are working separately to form communities focused on creating social change. This way of changing has an alluring aspect to it. During an interview with Talking Stick TV (2008), Carlsson described Nowtopia and the production that happens in it as "collaborative labor, derivative efforts, that base themselves as standing on the shoulders of those who came before us and producing beautiful new things" (8:45). This description sounds like a terrific way of creating things, but it must be much more complex than that.

> Nowtopians are not the beginning and end of social change, but they are an immanent part. Nowtopia is the fact that human beings are forever resilient in recreating patterns of behavior based on mutual aid, collaboration, and collective need, despite the forces working against those desires and impulses. (Carlsson & Manning, 2010, p. 951)

This concept of social action, being done through small networks and groups, is a powerful way of creating social change. It seems to make provisions for small examples of the change to take place and for those examples to happen from within the broader and current culture. Both of these aspects appear to make the concept of Nowtopia compelling.

Scalability is an important consideration when looking at change. Some interactions can work at a small scale (maybe within a small group of people), but new challenges that were not first considered arise when they become more broadly adopted. One way of thinking about this topic is related to software. It can be one thing to make software that works for one person. As you start to have more people use that software, they will likely have different needs or see other ways of doing things with that software. Many programmers and apps will have an extended beta period where they open up their software to be tested and used by a small subset of the final users. In some ways, these small groups of Nowtopians can beta test these new systems.

The aspect that these groups are being lived in daily life is also extremely persuasive. There is a significant benefit to using our imagination and working to envision how things could be different, as Meadows (1994) and Hopkins (2019) describe. This helps us make plans, gain motivation, and drive discussion. When we have a tangible example of a new way of living, it is even more impactful. It allows us to see that it can happen and is happening and make adjustments with real-world data. We can see how things work out and what types of structures or other systems are needed to keep them functioning.

During the interview with Talking Stick TV (2008), Carlsson makes a final encouragement about not having to change everything right away. It is about making changes for the better. If we can continue to make changes for the better progressively, we will have an overall progressive impact on our society. By producing beta testers for new ways of being in our society, we can start to flesh out how these new systems can be. It really becomes up to us to say what we can do to make things better and how we can get others involved.
