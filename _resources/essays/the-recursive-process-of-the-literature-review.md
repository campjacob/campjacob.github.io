In writing, I find that I frequently have to rewrite my papers a couple of times. Many of the classes at CIIS have an iterative writing process associated with them. We will write two or three shorter essays or participate in various group discussions, and then there will be a more extensive final report that incorporates the initial ones. These terminal articles still take a significant amount of time. I always start writing them by copy and pasting the material I've already written into a new document. I go line by line, rewriting all of it. It is these cumulative papers of which I am often the proudest.

One of the reasons my writing might feel improved after going through various iterative revisions is that my big ideas are more defined. Murray (1981) identified that there are many times the writer doesn't know the meaning of the thing they are writing until after they are done with the first draft. In my own experience, I will start thinking about writing something one way, and then I will change my presentation and description as I go over it again. I need that first draft to help reveal what I want to say. After I know what I want to say, I can start working on how I want to say it.

Recursion relates to "constituting a procedure that can repeat itself indefinitely" (Merriam-Webster, n.d., para. 2). I do need to go over things repeatedly, often multiple times, but there is also a time to stop and move on to the next project. It seems that some of the art of writing is about understanding where you are at in the process. The principles that Murray (1981) describes building his checklist on include: building on strengths, cutting what can be cut, recognizing that simplicity is best, and allowing the writing to tell you what to write. He describes reading through our writing considering the focus, form, and voice. It would appear reasonable after having completed that, we would be ready to move on. In actually writing my dissertation, I plan on having each section have its own little silo of text that I can go through and revise and have a "state" that it is in (e.g., 1st draft, reviewed, shared with the chair, etc.).

In considering the literature review and our second draft, I will be going back over it line by line to review it before submitting the final. I am looking forward to diving into the feedback I got and considering what changes I should be making.
