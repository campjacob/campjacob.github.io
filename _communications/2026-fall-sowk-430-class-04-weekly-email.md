## Unit Introduction and What You Will Learn

In Week 4, we are finishing our discussion of the history of social work. There is much more to the story of this profession's origins, but I hope you have a better understanding of its foundations. We will explore the story of the orphan trains. There are also forums about the code of ethics and how it has evolved from a couple of different viewpoints. Finally, I have a short lecture video where I share some of my experiences in South America. The agenda of the video is:

- Review week four content
- Experiences working at an orphanage

Learning objectives this week

- Be exposed to child welfare from an international perspective.
- Describe the history of the orphan trains, including why children were placed on them and how receiving families were chosen.
- Trace how social work ethics have changed over time, from the profession's early focus on the morality of clients to today's digital period.
- Identify significant changes in recent revisions of the NASW Code of Ethics, including the 2017 and 2021 updates, and areas where it may still need to grow.
- Begin to consider the ethical challenges technology creates for practice, such as consent, confidentiality, and boundaries.

**W-04 A-01: Asynchronous Class Engagement and Participation**

The expectation is that each of your replies will be substantive and provide meaningful perspectives, contributing to the forum's conversation and scholarship. They can relate to the prompts or build on conversations shared by peers. - The PBS episode on the orphan trains is the starting point for _Social Services in History, Vantage of The Orphan Trains_ ([Sec 0](https://myheritage.heritage.edu/ICS/Academics/SOWK/SOWK_430/2627_FA-SOWK_430-0/💻_W-04914-920.jnz?portlet=Group_Discussion_Forums&screen=PostView&screenType=change&id=d6fd1a12-3d08-4b13-9d2c-078f08bb0cfa) or [Sec 2](https://myheritage.heritage.edu/ICS/Academics/SOWK/SOWK_430/2627_FA-SOWK_430-2/💻_W-04914-920.jnz?portlet=Group_Discussion_Forums&screen=PostView&screenType=change&id=099a61a5-c5ae-4de0-ac30-38382e0371a6)), which asks why children were placed on the trains, how families were selected, and what safety nets now keep poverty from separating families.
