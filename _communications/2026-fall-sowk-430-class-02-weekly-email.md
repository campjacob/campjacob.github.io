## Unit Introduction and What You Will Learn

In week two of SOWK 430, we will be learning more about and discussing the history of social work. Social justice is a core value of social workers ([National Association of Social Workers, 2021](https://www.socialworkers.org/About/Ethics/Code-of-Ethics/Code-of-Ethics-English)). This week you get to watch a documentary about the history of social work. In addition, there are forums where you can discuss various social justice movements in the United States. I've also included many readings you can choose to read to help situate yourself within each movement. They are all really good, and you can find them at the bottom of the page as a resource. Because the documentary I have you watch is about an hour, I am not creating a lecture video for this week.

**Week 02 Objectives:**

- Develop foundational knowledge about the first hundred years of social work as a profession.
- Critically consider the history of the social work profession.
- Describe how social workers participated in social movements, and how those movements shaped the profession.
- Connect a historical movement to social work practice or community conditions today.
- Practice using APA in-text citations and reference entries in a scholarly discussion.

## Unit Resources

The lecture video this week is a documentary published by the Council on Social Work Education (CSWE) and the National Association of Social Workers (NASW), directed by Offenbach (2001). The documentary [Legacies of Social Change: 100 Years of Professional Social Work in the United States](https://youtu.be/a4VzRSnksmA) can be viewed on YouTube.

For this class, I'm not going to grade forums based on students' use of APA style. However, it is helpful to practice using the APA style, as it will help you as you continue in the social work program. In your forums, you should include [in-text citations](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/citations) and then for the [reference list entry](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/references), you can just write the word reference and list the entries. This is an excellent opportunity to practice looking up how to do these citations. You don't need to worry about making it have a hanging indent, but try to have all of the parts of the entries. You can see that I model this throughout this course as well.
