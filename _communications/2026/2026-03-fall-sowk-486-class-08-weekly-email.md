## Unit Introduction and What You Will Learn

Weeks eight and nine focus on how social workers conduct assessments. Before we can build relationships with our clients and find ways to support them, we must first understand the real problem. Week eight is asynchronous, with no in-person class. We will be examining our assessment process through chapters eight and nine of Hepworth et al. (2023). In the forums, you will discuss the chapters, reflect on implicit bias using the IMPLICIT model, explore screening tools such as the PHQ-9 and GAD-7, and test how observant you are with a short video. This week your teach back group should be working on their video (it is due Monday 10/19/26 at 5 PM), which you will share in the Week 09 forums. The agenda for my lecture video will be updated after it is posted.

Learning objectives this week include:

- Identify ethical and professional considerations when using diagnostic tools and clinical terminology as BSW-level practitioners.  
- Apply strengths-based and person-in-environment frameworks to assessment practices.  
- Evaluate common screening tools for their relevance and utility in social work practice.
- Reflect on how implicit bias and attention shape what we notice during an assessment.

The expectation is that each of your replies will be substantive and provide meaningful perspectives, contributing to the forum's conversation and scholarship. They can be related to the prompts or build on conversations shared by peers. There are _four forums_ for this week, and you are expected to make at least **3 replies** across any of the forums and read all of your peers' replies. These forums include the following:

- The [Chapter Eight and Nine Content Discussion](https://myheritage.heritage.edu/ICS/Academics/SOWK/SOWK_486W/2627_FA-SOWK_486W-1/💻_W-081012-1018.jnz?portlet=Group_Discussion_Forums&screen=PostView&screenType=change&id=ced286cb-8262-435a-88fa-447713b8e087) forum offers prompts from the textbook on diagnostic labels, culturally competent assessment, observation and self-monitoring as sources of information, mental status exams, spirituality, and suicide risk.
- After taking an Implicit Association Test, students reflect on how unconscious bias could shape their assessments and use the IMPLICIT model to counter it in [Exploring and Reflection on our Implicit Bias](https://myheritage.heritage.edu/ICS/Academics/SOWK/SOWK_486W/2627_FA-SOWK_486W-1/💻_W-081012-1018.jnz?portlet=Group_Discussion_Forums&screen=PostView&screenType=change&id=3fd57f01-22ae-43c6-bcd4-396f78fb77e0).
- [Screening Tools and Assessment Instruments](https://myheritage.heritage.edu/ICS/Academics/SOWK/SOWK_486W/2627_FA-SOWK_486W-1/💻_W-081012-1018.jnz?portlet=Group_Discussion_Forums&screen=PostView&screenType=change&id=1d9b23ac-1331-4848-bc2c-f7aaba6fef4e) invites students to explore published screeners such as the PHQ-9, GAD-7, and GAIN-SS, or to take the Perceived Stress Scale themselves and reflect on how useful a screener is for the worker and the client.
