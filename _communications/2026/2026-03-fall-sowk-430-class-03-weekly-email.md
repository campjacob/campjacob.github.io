## Unit Introduction and What You Will Learn

In week three, we are continuing to examine the history of social work and looking to gain insight into career options in social work. You are doing a great job so far in the course. We're going to continue with outside videos for the week. I also have a short lecture video for you to watch.

**Week Three Objectives**

- Further explore the Settlement House Movement
- Examine the broad range of career options for social workers
- Compare how social work is practiced across different fields of practice, populations, and levels of the system
- Locate and interpret current labor market information about the profession
- Consider what social work looks like in tribal, Indigenous, and rural communities in our own region

I'm encouraging people to consider attending the **State of Our Youth Symposium**. It will be a great event and you will learn a lot about the what trends look like for youth in our community. You can see the [event flier](https://cdnsm5-hosted.civiclive.com/UserFiles/Servers/Server_10765972/File/Child%20Adolescent%20Health/SoOY%20Registration%20Flyer.pdf) or the [BFHD Website](https://www.bfhd.wa.gov/health_topics/health_development/healthy_teens/state_of_our_youth_symposium), but the details are as follows:

📅 Date: September 29, 2026  
⏰ Time: 8:00 AM–12:00 PM  
📍 Location: Three Rivers Convention Center  

[Register for the State of Our Youth Symposium](https://outlook.office.com/book/BookingBFHDParentChildHealth@bfhd.wa.gov/s/65CtZ1cUYE-qC3y0MjSyBQ2?ismsaljsauthenabled) and I hope to see some of you there.

The website for the National Association of Social Workers (NASW) is a wealth of information. Students can consider [membership with the NASW](https://www.socialworkers.org/Membership), but all of the resources used this week are offered free by our professional network. I am a member of the NASW and the [Washington State School Social Workers Association](https://www.wassw.org).

Most of the images in this week's forums come from NASW's [Social Work Month — Logos & Infographics](https://www.socialworkers.org/News/Social-Work-Month/Logos-Infographics) page, which has the whole "Types of Social Workers" set in one place. NASW also keeps practice hubs with much deeper material on [areas of practice](https://www.socialworkers.org/Practice), and members can join [special practice sections](https://www.socialworkers.org/Careers/Specialty-Practice-Sections).

For the jobs and wages side of the week, the two places to start are the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics [Occupational Outlook Handbook entry for Social Workers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/community-and-social-service/social-workers.htm) and the [May 2025 OEWS Occupation Profiles](https://www.bls.gov/oes/2025/may/oes_stru.htm). Job boards worth knowing are [Idealist](https://www.idealist.org) (nonprofit-focused), [NASW JobLink](https://www.socialworkers.org/Careers/NASW-JobLink) (our profession's own board), and the general boards like [Indeed](https://www.indeed.com) and [Monster](https://www.monster.com).
